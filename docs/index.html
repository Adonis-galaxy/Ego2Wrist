Video

Wrist views from egocentric video

Wrist cameras provide an action-centric view that is directly relevant to robot manipulation.

However, collecting human wrist-camera videos at scale is challenging, as the camera setup is cumbersome and can interfere with natural hand movements.

In contrast, head-mounted human egocentric videos are much easier to scale.

Synthesizing wrist views from these videos could transform scalable real-world human demonstrations into action-centric data for robot policy training.

Ego2Wrist

Ego2Wrist turns head-camera video into wrist-camera video and improves itself on video that has no wrist cameras.

Recursive self-improvement: round 1 → round 2 → …

→ →

(1) The model is trained on recordings with head and wrist cameras. (2) It then generates both wrist videos for in-the-wild egocentric video from Egocentric-10K, which has only a head camera. (3) HCC compares each generated sample with the region around the hand in the head view and rejects samples that disagree. (4) Training continues on the accepted samples, and the improved model generates the next round.

Ego2Wrist has the lowest LPIPS on every dataset (standard test set, 375 clips). LPIPS compares the generated with the real wrist videos (lower is better). In a human study with the real wrist videos shown, people prefer Ego2Wrist over WorldWander in 76% of answers.

HCC agrees with people as well as LPIPS does, although it never sees the real wrist video

Hand-centric cross-view consistency (HCC) is a ground-truth-free metric that measures whether generated wrist views are consistent with the corresponding hand regions in the input head view.

The region around the same-side hand in the head view (dashed) is matched with the generated wrist view; lines show the strongest DINOv3 patch matches.

People chose the better of two generated videos from different checkpoints (407 answers); the bars show how well each score predicts their choice (Spearman correlation). Dashed line: agreement between two people.
